### **Bra–Ket Notation Convention**

Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a complex Hilbert space with scalar inner product $\langle\cdot\mid\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}$. Our convention extends Dirac notation to $\mathcal{H}$-valued matrices.

* **Ket / right vector:** $|e\rangle \in \mathcal{H}$.
* **Bra / left vector:** $\langle e| := (|e\rangle)^*$, where $*$ denotes the conjugate transpose / adjoint.
* Thus $\langle e|f\rangle = \langle e, f\rangle_{\mathcal{H}} \in \mathbb{C}$.

For an indexed vector family $e_{1..n} = (e_1, \dots, e_n)$:

* **Ket row:** $|e_{1..n}\rangle := (|e_1\rangle, \dots, |e_n\rangle) \in M_{1,n}(\mathcal H)\cong \mathcal B(\mathbb C^n,\mathcal H)$.
* **Bra column:** $\langle e_{1..n}| := (|e_{1..n}\rangle)^* = (\langle e_1|, \dots, \langle e_n|)^{\mathrm{T}} \in M_{n,1}(\mathcal H^*)\cong \mathcal B(\mathcal H,\mathbb C^n)$.
* The index notation $1..n$ is preserved under bra/ket conversion; the transpose is already encoded by $*$.
* Hence $\langle e_{1..n}|e_{1..n}\rangle = (\langle e_i|e_j\rangle_{\mathcal{H}})_{i,j}$, the **Gram matrix**.

More generally, for an $\mathcal{H}$-valued matrix $E = (E_{ij}) \in M_{m,n}(\mathcal{H})$:

* **Ket matrix:** $|E\rangle := (|E_{ij}\rangle)_{i,j}$.
* **Bra matrix:** $\langle E| := |E\rangle^*$.
* For $E, F \in M_{m,n}(\mathcal{H})$, define the **matrix-valued bra–ket product** $\langle E|F\rangle := |E\rangle^* |F\rangle \in M_n(\mathbb{C})$.
* Entrywise, $(\langle E|F\rangle)_{jk} = \sum_{i=1}^m \langle E_{ij}|F_{ik}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}$.

The vector-family case is simply the special case $m = 1$.

For $\boldsymbol{A} \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times p}$,

$$
\langle e_{1..n}\boldsymbol{A}| := (|e_{1..n}\rangle \boldsymbol{A})^* = \boldsymbol{A}^* \langle e_{1..n}|.
$$

Hence, for $\boldsymbol{A} \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times p}$ and $\boldsymbol{B} \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times q}$,

$$
\langle e_{1..n}\boldsymbol{A}|e_{1..n}\boldsymbol{B}\rangle = \boldsymbol{A}^* \langle e_{1..n}|e_{1..n}\rangle \boldsymbol{B}.
$$

Writing $\boldsymbol{G}_e := \langle e_{1..n}|e_{1..n}\rangle$, this is simply

$$
\langle e_{1..n}\boldsymbol{A}|e_{1..n}\boldsymbol{B}\rangle = \boldsymbol{A}^* \boldsymbol{G}_e \boldsymbol{B}.
$$

If $e_{1..n}$ is orthonormal, $\boldsymbol{G}_e = \boldsymbol{I}_n$, so the result is $\boldsymbol{A}^* \boldsymbol{B}$.

Let $A \in \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$ be a linear operator and $e, f \in \mathcal{H}$.

* **Operator acting on a ket:** $A|f\rangle := |Af\rangle$.
* **Bra–operator:** $\langle e|A := (A^*|e\rangle)^* = \langle A^*e|$.
* **Bra–operator–ket / matrix element:** $\langle e|A|f\rangle := \langle e, Af\rangle_{\mathcal{H}} \in \mathbb{C}$.
* Hence $\langle e|A|f\rangle^* = \langle f|A^*|e\rangle$.
* When $e = f$, $\langle e|A|e\rangle$ is the **expectation value** of $A$ in the state $e$ (for normalized $e$).

For vector families $e_{1..m}$ and $f_{1..n}$,

$$
\langle e_{1..m}|A|f_{1..n}\rangle := (\langle e_i|A|f_j\rangle)_{i,j} \in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C}).
$$

In particular, for a basis $e_{1..n}$,

$$
[A]_e = \langle e_{1..n}|A|e_{1..n}\rangle
$$

when the basis is orthonormal. Thus the ordinary matrix representation of an operator is literally its **bra–operator–ket matrix**.

Let $e_{1..n}$ be an orthonormal basis and let $[\mathcal{A}]_e = \boldsymbol{G}$. Then

$$
|e_{1..n}\boldsymbol{y}\rangle = |e_{1..n}\rangle\boldsymbol{y}, \qquad \langle e_{1..n}\boldsymbol{x}| = \boldsymbol{x}^* \langle e_{1..n}|.
$$

Since $\mathcal{A}|e_{1..n}\rangle = |e_{1..n}\rangle\boldsymbol{G}$,

$$
\langle e_{1..n}\boldsymbol{x}|\mathcal{A}|e_{1..n}\boldsymbol{y}\rangle = \boldsymbol{x}^* \langle e_{1..n}|e_{1..n}\rangle \boldsymbol{G}\boldsymbol{y}.
$$

Orthonormality gives $\langle e_{1..n}|e_{1..n}\rangle = \boldsymbol{I}$, hence

$$
\langle e_{1..n}\boldsymbol{x}|\mathcal{A}|e_{1..n}\boldsymbol{y}\rangle = \boldsymbol{x}^* \boldsymbol{G}\boldsymbol{y}.
$$

Define the ket space by

$$
\mathbf{Kets}(X) := \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{F}, X) \cong X, \qquad \mathbb{F} = \mathbb{R} \text{ or } \mathbb{C}.
$$

A ket $|x\rangle_X$ is the rank-one operator $|x\rangle_X : \lambda \mapsto \lambda x$.

Let  be Hilbert spaces. Define the **ket constructor**  and the **bra constructor** , with ; over , the bra constructor is conjugate-linear.

For $A_{X \leftarrow Y} : Y \to X$ and $x \in X, \, y \in Y$,

$$
A_{X \leftarrow Y} |y\rangle_Y = |Ay\rangle_X \quad \text{and} \quad {}_X\langle x| A_{X \leftarrow Y} = {}_Y\langle A^* x|.
$$

Hence the **matrix element** is ${}_X\langle x| A_{X \leftarrow Y} |y\rangle_Y = \langle x, Ay\rangle_X \in \mathbb{C}$.

The reverse composition gives the **outer product** $|x\rangle_{XY}\langle y| \in \mathcal{B}(Y, X)$, with $(|x\rangle_{XY}\langle y|)|z\rangle_Y = |x\rangle_X \langle y|z\rangle_Y$.

For basis families $e_{1..m} \subset X, \, f_{1..n} \subset Y$, ${}_X\langle e_{1..m}| A_{X \leftarrow Y} |f_{1..n}\rangle_Y \in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C})$.

Take $X = \mathbb{R}^{d_x}, \, Y = \mathbb{R}^{d_y}$, and $A_{X \leftarrow Y} \leftrightarrow \boldsymbol{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_x \times d_y}$.

For $u \in X, \, v \in Y$, $|u\rangle_X = \boldsymbol{u} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_x \times 1}, \, {}_X\langle u| = \boldsymbol{u}^{\mathrm{T}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d_x}$, and $|v\rangle_Y = \boldsymbol{v} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_y \times 1}$.

Thus $A_{X \leftarrow Y}|v\rangle_Y = \boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{v} \in X$, while ${}_X\langle u| A_{X \leftarrow Y} = \boldsymbol{u}^{\mathrm{T}}\boldsymbol{A} \in Y^*$.

Finally, ${}_X\langle u| A_{X \leftarrow Y} |v\rangle_Y = \boldsymbol{u}^{\mathrm{T}}\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{v} \in \mathbb{R}$, and since $\boldsymbol{u}^{\mathrm{T}}\boldsymbol{A} = (\boldsymbol{A}^{\mathrm{T}}\boldsymbol{u})^{\mathrm{T}}$, we have ${}_X\langle u| A_{X \leftarrow Y} = {}_Y\langle A^*_{X \leftarrow Y}u|$.

In an abstract Hilbert space, introduce the Riesz map

$$
* : \mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{H}^*, \qquad e \mapsto e^* := \langle e|,
$$

so $e^*(v) := \langle e, v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}$. Then immediately

$$
e_i^* e_j := e_i^*(e_j) = \langle e_i, e_j\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.
$$

For the matrix Hilbert space $M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C})$,

$$
\langle A, B\rangle_F = \operatorname{tr}(A^* B),
$$

so the same idea becomes

$$
A^* : B \mapsto \operatorname{tr}(A^* B).
$$
